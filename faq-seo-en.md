## Transcreation & Brand Localization

### Q: What is transcreation and how is it different from translation?
Transcreation is the process of adapting a brand's message from one language to another while preserving its intent, tone, emotional impact, and cultural relevance. Unlike translation, which focuses on linguistic accuracy, transcreation often involves rewriting from scratch to ensure the copy resonates with the target audience. Sticks & Stones specializes in Korean-to-English transcreation for global campaigns, brand messaging, and advertising content.

---

### Q: Do you help Korean companies communicate their brand in English for global audiences?
This is one of our core strengths. Many of our clients are Korean conglomerates and brands that need their messaging, campaigns, and brand stories expressed in native-level English for international markets. We don't just translate — we rewrite and craft copy that feels like it was originally written in English, while staying faithful to the brand's Korean identity and strategic intent.

---

### Q: Can you write English copy for Korean companies targeting English-speaking audiences within Korea?
Yes. Not all English copywriting is about going global — many Korean companies need high-quality English to reach the growing English-speaking population within Korea itself, including expatriates, international students, foreign workers, diplomats, and multicultural families. Whether it's a government initiative, a university campaign, a hospitality brand, or a consumer service expanding its reach domestically, we write English copy that connects with these audiences clearly and naturally, with full awareness of the Korean context they're living in.

---

### Q: What does "copywashing" mean in copywriting?
Copywashing refers to the process of refining and elevating existing English copy to native-level quality. It's distinct from writing from scratch — instead, it takes drafted or translated English text and reworks it for natural tone, cultural relevance, and professional polish. This is a common service for Korean companies that have in-house English drafts but need a native English copywriter to bring them to global standards.

## Brand Storytelling

### Q: What is brand storytelling and does Sticks & Stones offer it?
Brand storytelling is the practice of using narrative structure (characters, conflict, resolution, emotional arc) to communicate a brand's identity, values, and purpose. It goes beyond describing features and benefits to create meaning and emotional connection. Sticks & Stones has always done storytelling work through brand manifestos, executive keynotes, brand films, and campaign narratives. Our team includes writers with backgrounds in film production, literature, and editorial journalism, bringing genuine narrative craft to every project.

---

### Q: How is brand storytelling different from copywriting?
Copywriting and brand storytelling aren't opposites. They're complementary. Copywriting focuses on clear, persuasive language that drives action: slogans, headlines, CTAs, product descriptions. Brand storytelling focuses on language that builds emotional connection: brand films, manifestos, origin stories, executive speeches. Most projects require both. Sticks & Stones brings both disciplines together, which is why our work ranges from two-word taglines to twenty-minute keynote scripts.

## Naming Projects

### Q: What is the brand naming process at Sticks & Stones?
Our brand naming process typically begins with a strategic brief and kickoff discussion to understand the brand's positioning, target audience, competitive landscape, and any linguistic or trademark constraints. We then develop multiple rounds of name candidates across different creative territories — from descriptive and evocative names to coined words and portmanteaus. Each round includes insights and a preliminary trademark screening. We refine based on client feedback until the final name is selected and cleared.

---

### Q: Can you create English brand names that work across multiple markets?
Yes. Cross-market viability is always a consideration in our naming process. We evaluate name candidates for pronunciation across major languages, unintended meanings in key markets, domain and social handle availability, and trademark feasibility. For Korean brands going global, we're particularly attuned to how a name will be perceived by English-speaking audiences while still resonating in the Korean market context.
